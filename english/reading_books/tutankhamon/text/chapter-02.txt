CHAPTER 2 The Ceremonie
There was a lot of excitement in the streets.
The people waved and smiled at Tut.
All these people are looking at me, thought Tut.
He was frightened and he wanted to go home.
Thousands of men, women and children filled the stadium.
The government ministers sat in the front seats.
The servants carried the chariots to the royal seats.
You're the pharaoh and you must sit on your father's throne now, said Queen Kia.
Tut felt very small on the big throne.
Fortunately, I, the pharaoh's chief advisor, was next to him.
I kissed Tut's hand but Tut pulled it from him.
You've got a lot to learn, young pharaoh.
I said.
But don't worry, we'll start your lessons tomorrow.
Trumpets played and everybody stood for the national anthem.
Then the ceremony began.
At first, some ministers made long speeches and it was very boring.
Then soldiers came into the stadium on chariots.
They carried bows and arrows and there were feathers on the horse's heads.
The commander of the Egyptian army, Horamhab, rode in front.
Tutankhamun didn't like Horamhab.
He always looked serious or angry.
After the parade, dancers performed in front of the royal seats.
Then the games began.
There was a javelin competition, a chariot race and a marathon.
But Tut's favourite event was the archery competition.
Finally, Tut met the winners and gave medals to all of them.
He was very excited.
Maybe it will be fun to be a pharaoh after all, he thought.
